# Fresh Amnesiac Critic — Living Painting Evidence Review

**Role:** I am reviewing this evidence cold. I have not read the code, the
mission contract, or the donor archaeology. I only see the 11 screenshots
and the storyboard. My job is to answer one question honestly:

> Does this look like a "living painting" — or does it look like a GPU demo?

---

## What I See

Eleven screenshots of warm amber/cream particles on a dark brown-black
background. The particles are soft-edged, varied in size, and cluster in an
organic cloud shape with a dark void at the center.

## What Works

1. **The palette is painterly.** The warm amber-cream-umber color range reads
   like aged oil pigment or candlelit varnish. It does not look like a neon
   tech demo. The gradient-map recoloring earns its keep.

2. **The pointer response is the hero moment.** The ring formation when the
   pointer is at center (S3_pointer-center) is visually striking. The
   asymmetric scatter when the pointer moves to the corner shows emergent
   behavior — these particles are not following a script, they are reacting.
   This is the strongest evidence of "living" behavior.

3. **Temporal motion is real.** Comparing S2 frames, the mass is visibly
   shifting, not static. The silhouette changes shape organically between
   frames. This is breathing, not ticking.

4. **Size variation prevents uniformity.** The jittered particle sizes create
   visual rhythm — larger dabs in the slow core, smaller scouts at the edges.
   This is a small thing that matters a lot for painterly feel.

## What Does Not Work (Yet)

1. **There is no painting.** The particles float in a void. There is no canvas
   texture, no underlying image, no frame, no wall. A "living painting" needs
   a painting to live on. Right now this is a living cloud of paint dabs on
   darkness. It's beautiful, but it's abstract.

2. **The dark void is prominent.** The central obstacle creates a black hole
   in the center of the composition. Combined with the empty surrounding
   space, the overall impression is: particle donut on black background. In
   motion this is less noticeable, but in stills it stands out.

3. **The particles still read as particles, not brushstrokes.** They are
   soft circles, not elongated marks. A brushstroke has direction, varied
   width, texture variation along its length. These are paint dabs — better
   than dots, but not yet strokes. The direction-field capability exists
   in the code but its visual influence is not visible in the evidence.

4. **Composition uses only ~40% of the canvas.** The entire particle cloud
   sits in the center third, leaving the edges completely dark and empty.
   This reinforces the "floating demo" feel rather than "a painting that
   fills the frame."

5. **No depth or layering.** All particles appear to be on the same visual
   plane. Paint on canvas has thick/thin, over/under, wet/dry. Here
   everything blends flatly.

## Verdict

**CONDITIONAL PASS as a capability proof. NOT READY as a product claim.**

The extracted capabilities work. The flocking is real, the interaction is
responsive, the palette is warm. As a Stone 4 "integration proof," this
demonstrates that the four sculpted capabilities (GPGPU boids, direction field,
Poisson seeds, gradient-map) can combine into something that moves, responds,
and looks painterly.

But "Living Painting" as a product claim needs a painting. What I see is a
living particle cloud. The jump from "particle cloud with warm palette" to
"painting that breathes" requires:

- An actual artwork image underneath (or a procedurally generated one)
- Brushstroke shapes driven by the direction field (the capability exists
  but is not visually expressed)
- Canvas texture or surface treatment
- Full-frame composition

These are all Stone 5+ integration concerns, not capability extraction failures.

**Critic recommendation:** KEEP the capability proof. Do NOT claim this is a
"Living Painting product" yet. Label it honestly: "Living Paint Cloud —
capability integration proof." The capabilities are transferable and the
interaction is genuinely compelling. The painting part comes when these
particles dress an actual artwork.

---

ring is legitimately good, the palette works, but the "painting" claim needs
the painting. A human reviewer should focus on the S3 pointer-center shot as
the best evidence of what the capability can do.
