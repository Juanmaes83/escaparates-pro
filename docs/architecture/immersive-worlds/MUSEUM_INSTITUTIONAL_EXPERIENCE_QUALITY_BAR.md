# Immersive Worlds — Museum / Institutional Experience Quality Bar

> **Status:** PRODUCT DIRECTION / QUALITY DOCTRINE — to be used by future Museum / Institutional work.
> **Scope:** raises the creative and experiential quality bar without replacing IW-0 or invalidating IW-1.
> **Reference role:** TheVertMenthe is a visual/interaction quality reference, not a template to copy.

---

## 1. Where we are

IW-1 demonstrates:

> **We can build the system.**

It proves the architectural foundation: semantic World/Space/Entity data, canonical records, anchors, portals, hotspots, actions, camera authority, Explore, Guided Experience, Scene Kit separation, thin authoring and deterministic QA.

That foundation must be protected.

The next challenge is different:

> **The system must be capable of disappearing so that only the experience remains.**

The Museum / Institutional vertical must evolve from a technically credible 3D prototype into a digital exhibition that feels authored, coherent and spatially intentional.

---

## 2. Product-level objective

Museum / Institutional is not merely a “virtual museum builder”.

The stronger product direction is:

> **A platform capable of turning a collection, institution or exhibition into a spatial digital experience with its own art direction and experience language.**

Long-term conceptual flow:

```text
CREATE WORLD
→ MUSEUM / INSTITUTION

CHOOSE EXPERIENCE LANGUAGE
→ WHITE CUBE
→ HERITAGE
→ EDITORIAL
→ DARK EXHIBITION
→ CUSTOM BRAND DNA

IMPORT COLLECTION
→ DEFINE SPACES
→ PLACE CONTENT
→ DEFINE CURATORIAL JOURNEY
→ DEFINE FOCUS LANGUAGE
→ DEFINE SOUND
→ PUBLISH
```

This is a product vision, not an instruction to implement every capability immediately. MUST V1 / SHOULD LATER / R&D discipline still applies.

---

## 3. Governing experience principle

The visitor should not feel that they are operating a 3D application.

They should feel that they have entered an exhibition.

> **THE EXPERIENCE IS THE INTERFACE.**

The world should explain itself through:

- spatial composition;
- architecture;
- artwork placement;
- movement;
- camera;
- signage;
- sound;
- visual hierarchy;
- interaction affordances.

UI exists when useful, but it must not dominate the experience.

Avoid defaulting to:

- SaaS-like panels;
- permanent floating cards;
- generic game HUDs;
- futuristic overlays;
- gratuitous glassmorphism;
- controls that sit visually outside the exhibition language.

---

## 4. TheVertMenthe — what matters

TheVertMenthe demonstrates a coherent digital gallery with:

- strong spatial perspective;
- architectural rhythm;
- consistent framing of artworks;
- minimal interface;
- spatial navigation;
- proximity and inspection;
- authored focus states;
- an optional sense of embodied presence;
- one unified visual and experiential language.

Its monochrome illustrative aesthetic is **not** the target.

What matters is that architecture, artwork, typography, movement, camera, interaction and UI all belong to one designed universe.

Do not copy the visual identity. Understand why the experience feels coherent.

---

## 5. Museum Scene Kit ≠ a skin system

A Museum Scene Kit must support more than changes to colors, materials and lights.

The intended conceptual direction is:

```text
MUSEUM SCENE KIT
+
EXPERIENCE LANGUAGE / ART DIRECTION PROFILE
```

Possible Experience Languages include:

- WHITE CUBE
- HERITAGE
- EDITORIAL
- DARK EXHIBITION
- BRUTALIST
- ARCHIVAL
- ILLUSTRATED
- SCULPTURAL
- CUSTOM BRAND DNA

These must not be treated as superficial skins.

An Experience Language may influence:

- spatial composition;
- architecture character;
- materials;
- lighting language;
- typography;
- signage;
- content framing;
- focus treatment;
- navigation treatment;
- transition language;
- sound character;
- motion character;
- presence profile;
- rhythm and pacing.

Core principle:

> **SAME SEMANTICS, DIFFERENT AUTHORED EXPERIENCE.**

`Experience Language` is currently a product/design concept, not a required new runtime subsystem or schema. Do not prematurely formalize it unless implementation evidence justifies that step.

---

## 6. Architecture is composition, not just geometry

A technically valid room is not enough.

Museum spaces must be judged through:

```text
SPATIAL COMPOSITION
VISUAL RHYTHM
FOCAL POINTS
SCALE
SEQUENCE
DEPTH
PAUSE
REVEAL
```

The difference is:

```text
IW-1:
“there is a room here”

QUALITY BAR:
“this is how the visitor should feel when entering this room”
```

Walls, floor and ceiling are infrastructure. Art direction determines what the space communicates.

---

## 7. Content must dominate technology

WebGL technology is infrastructure, not the subject.

Do not attempt to signal quality through gratuitous:

- bloom;
- particles;
- neon;
- decorative shaders;
- excessive reflections;
- holographic language;
- technical spectacle disconnected from content.

The visitor should remember:

- the space;
- the works;
- the route;
- the atmosphere;
- the story;
- the institution.

Not “Three.js”.

> **IF THE TECHNOLOGY ATTRACTS MORE ATTENTION THAN THE CONTENT, THE MUSEUM SCENE KIT IS FAILING.**

---

## 8. Focus is temporary staging, not a card

Focus is one of the most important experience states.

Desired grammar:

```text
EXPLORE
→ APPROACH
→ SELECT
→ FOCUS
→ ARTWORK DOMINATES FRAME
→ MINIMAL CONTEXT
→ RETURN
```

Focus should be an authored temporary mise-en-scène.

It may involve:

- intentional camera recomposition;
- artwork scale/presence becoming dominant;
- background de-emphasis where appropriate;
- integrated metadata;
- sound or narration;
- controlled return to the original spatial state.

Avoid reducing Focus to:

```text
CLICK
→ OPEN GENERIC CARD
```

The existing IW contract is appropriate:

```text
ENTITY
→ semantic bounds / anchors
→ FOCUS CAMERA
→ SCENE KIT PRESENTATION
```

The Museum Scene Kit should exploit that capability much more ambitiously.

---

## 9. Information belongs to the exhibition language

Metadata must support the artwork rather than compete with it.

The question is not:

> “Which card do we show?”

The question is:

> **“How is this information presented inside the museographic language of this experience?”**

Possible treatments include:

- integrated signage;
- spatial typography;
- minimal overlay;
- focus caption;
- exhibition label;
- editorial layer;
- audio;
- guided narration.

The current IW-1 side panel is functionally useful, but should not be assumed to be the final visual grammar.

---

## 10. Navigation has art direction

Navigation UI is not automatically a generic cross-vertical HUD.

Conceptually:

```text
NAVIGATION UI
=
DIGITAL SIGNAGE / EXPERIENCE LANGUAGE
```

The World Engine owns navigation semantics. The Scene Kit / Experience Language decides how those semantics are expressed visually and behaviorally.

Controls should feel native to the exhibition.

---

## 11. Sound is spatial design

Sound must not be reduced to “play audio file”.

Museum / Institutional sound may include:

- ambience;
- silence;
- room tone;
- narration;
- proximity cues;
- sound artworks;
- transitions;
- guided journey cues.

Sound should collaborate with architecture, focus and narrative.

---

## 12. Presence is a design dimension

TheVertMenthe demonstrates that a minimal avatar can add:

- scale;
- presence;
- identity.

This does **not** promote avatars into the current critical path.

The architecture should remain compatible with future Presence Profiles such as:

```text
CAMERA ONLY
FIRST PERSON
MINIMAL AVATAR
CUSTOM AVATAR
GUIDE CHARACTER
```

without rebuilding World Engine.

Presence is a product/design dimension, not automatically a V1 feature.

---

## 13. Custom Brand DNA — product destination

Museum / Institutional should eventually support institutional identity beyond:

- logo;
- color;
- font.

Potential Brand DNA dimensions include:

- architecture language;
- materials;
- graphic system;
- typography;
- signage;
- motion;
- content treatment;
- sound;
- camera behaviour.

This is a medium/long-term product destination, not an excuse for scope explosion in IW-2.

---

## 14. Protect the engine from visual impatience

Before changing World Engine because the experience does not yet look good enough, first test whether the issue can be solved in:

1. Museum Scene Kit;
2. art direction;
3. spatial composition;
4. materials;
5. lighting;
6. focus presentation;
7. signage/UI;
8. motion;
9. sound.

Only propose a core architectural change when there is real evidence that the engine blocks the required experience.

Do not convert every visual problem into a new core feature.

## 15. Museum / Institutional quality tests

These tests are conceptual quality gates and should be used in future visual review, Gauntlet comparisons and implementation critique.

### MUSEUM PRESENCE TEST

A capture of the space without secondary UI should look like an authored experience, not a technical blockout.

Question:

> **Would I want to enter this space?**

### ARTWORK DOMINANCE TEST

When an artwork enters Focus:

- the artwork becomes protagonist;
- camera helps;
- information accompanies it;
- UI does not compete.

Question:

> **Am I looking at the artwork, or am I operating an interface?**

### SPATIAL IDENTITY TEST

The same semantic World rendered through two Experience Languages should feel genuinely different without changing its semantic content.

Question:

> **Do we have a representation system, or only a skin?**

For early milestones, this may be demonstrated on a limited scene/subset rather than by building two complete museums.

### UI DISAPPEARANCE TEST

If secondary UI is removed:

- the experience remains understandable;
- the space remains usable;
- the main navigation still makes sense.

Question:

> **Does the world explain the experience?**

### INSTITUTIONAL COHERENCE TEST

Architecture + artwork + lighting + typography + signage + interaction + sound should feel like one creative direction.

Question:

> **Does this feel like a real authored exhibition/institution, or a collection of components?**

## 16. IW-2 objective

IW-2 should be treated as:

> **MUSEUM QUALITY & FOUNDATION VALIDATION GATE**

The purpose is not to add twenty features.

The purpose is to demonstrate that the architecture validated in IW-1 can produce a strong authored experience without breaking its contracts.

Primary areas:

```text
ART DIRECTION
SPATIAL COMPOSITION
MATERIALS
LIGHTING
FOCUS EXPERIENCE
NAVIGATION EXPERIENCE
SIGNAGE / UI
SOUND
MOBILE
PERFORMANCE
ACCESSIBILITY
GAUNTLET
UNSLOP
```

Maintain zero regression in IW-1 architectural invariants.

Do not use IW-2 to prematurely add:

- new verticals;
- advanced editor;
- global navigation integration;
- avatar system;
- AI curator;
- large new core subsystems;
- merge to `master`.

## 18. Final doctrine

> **The visitor should not feel that they are using a 3D application. They should feel that they have entered an exhibition.**

And:

> **IW-1 demonstrates that we can build the system. The next quality bar is proving that the system can disappear and leave only the experience.**

The product distinction is:

```text
NOT ONLY:
BUILDING A 3D MUSEUM

BUT:
BUILDING A SYSTEM CAPABLE OF CREATING DIGITAL EXHIBITIONS
WITH THEIR OWN ART DIRECTION AND EXPERIENCE LANGUAGE
```

That second definition is the Museum / Institutional product direction.
