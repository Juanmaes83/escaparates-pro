IW-1 proved the architecture. IW-2 makes the Museum vertical look and behave like

walls, a building rather than a box, and a detail state worth entering.

---

## 1. What changed, and why it was chosen

The brief asked for a clearly higher level of quality and closeness to the product
goal. Four things were selected because each one blocks the product claim, not
because they were the easiest wins.

### 1.1 Content is now supplied, not synthesised — the product-defining change

IW-1 generated every plate in the browser. That proved the fallback path and
proved nothing commercially: an institution's whole reason to buy this is to see
**its own collection** on the walls.

- `content.media` is now part of the semantic schema:
  `{ kind: IMAGE | VIDEO | AUDIO | GENERATED, src, poster, aspect, credit, rights }`.
- `render/media-loader.js` resolves it — with a timeout, a reference-counted
  texture cache, and video through a real `<video>` element.
- **A broken file never leaves a hole in a wall.** A failed load degrades to the
  generated plate and emits `asset:error`. QA asserts this against a deliberately
  missing file.
- **The validator refuses media that declares no rights** (`INV-10`). An
  institution putting a file on a wall must be able to say who owns it. This is a
  product decision expressed as a schema constraint.

The Fundación Arenas collection now ships as seven real files — six JPEGs and one
WebM — generated by our own plate generator at print resolution and committed
under `assets/collection/`, with `RIGHTS.md` recording that they are ours and
that nothing third-party is involved. `qa/tools/make-assets.mjs` regenerates them.

Swap the files, keep the world definition, and it is a different exhibition.

### 1.2 The gallery is a building now

The reference image's single strongest signal is the roof. A flat lid reads as a
box no matter how good the materials are.

- **Pitched roof with skylight bays** along the ridge, built as alternating solid
  ribs and glazing in the upper half of each slope — how a real roof light is
  framed, and no CSG needed.
- **Daylight through the roof**, raking rather than falling straight down.
- **Cornice**, and a bounce light so the roof underside is lit by the floor
  rather than going dead.
- **Stanchion-and-rope barrier lines**, derived from where work actually hangs:
  an author places a painting and the room protects it. The rope sags on a
  quadratic curve, because a straight rope reads as a fence.
- **One hanging line**: work centres align at 1.55 m, museum convention.
- Works are larger, and the roof profile is per-profile and per-space
  overridable (`space.metadata.roof`), so the dark room stays flat and lightless.

### 1.3 Focus became a detail state worth entering

IW-1's focus was a camera move plus a small side panel. Now:

- the room **dims from the edges inward** so the work stays the brightest thing;
- a **typeset panel** carries title, attribution, curatorial text, dimensions,
  location, reference and image credit — all read from the record, so a client
  who fills in more fields gets a richer panel with no interface work;
- **inspection zoom** dollies toward the surface rather than narrowing the lens —
  the difference between examining a painting and magnifying a photograph of it;
- **previous / next** step through the works of the room without walking back,
  through the ordinary `FOCUS_ENTITY` action, so the trace in World State is
  identical to walking up to each one;
- the **camera framing composes into the visible area**, offsetting laterally by
  the fraction of the viewport the panel covers. Without this the panel sat on
  top of the painting it was describing.

### 1.4 Configurability is demonstrated, not asserted

`worlds/institutional-demo.world.json` is a second, complete world — different
institution, different architecture, different route, four works — running on the
same engine, the same Scene Kit and the same assets. Open it with
`?world=./worlds/institutional-demo.world.json`.

Building it immediately found two real defects, which is exactly what a second
world is for (see §3).

## 2. Reference library: audited, and used where legal

`REFERENCE_REUSE_ACCELERATION_POLICY.md` says search the library first and reuse
boldly when legal. Doing that properly meant establishing what "legal" is. Full
audit in `REFERENCE_REUSE_REGISTER.md`; the outcome:

| Repository | Licence | Direct reuse in a commercial product |
|---|---|---|
| `artwork-3D-museum` | CC BY-NC 4.0 (© TomPast) | ❌ NonCommercial |
| `3D-art-gallery-threejs` | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (© Emilian Kasemi) | ❌ NonCommercial + ShareAlike |
| `3DArtMuseum` | GPL-3.0 | ❌ Copyleft |
| `portfolio-itom-and-advanced-WebGL` | **MIT** (© Tomasz Szmajda) | ✅ **Yes**, with attribution |

The three repositories the Ledger names as *primary* for Museum cannot have their
code copied into a product that bills customers. They are also forks — the
copyright holders are not Juanma — which is the trap the policy names in §10.
For them, patterns-only is not caution; it is the only lawful mode.

**The MIT one paid for the whole exercise.** Reading its `RoomWarmup.jsx` exposed
a real bug in our code:

> IW-1 built each Space hidden and then called `compileAsync`. Three.js compiles
> what `traverseVisible` reaches — **our warmup was compiling nothing**, and the
> entire shader cost still landed on the first frame the visitor saw. QA had not
> caught it because it only checked that warm time was above zero.

Fixed, plus three more evidence-backed mechanisms adapted from the same source:
skip warmup on LOW to avoid context loss, disable shadows on mobile regardless of
tier, and render below native resolution on LOW. All attributed in code and in the
register.

## 3. Defects this milestone found and fixed

Worth listing, because each was invisible until something forced it into the open.

1. **Warmup compiled nothing** (above) — found by reading an MIT reference.
2. **Escape did not work in focus mode.** Input was gated by one flag tied to
   camera authority, so entering focus disabled the very key that leaves it. The
   visitor was trapped; QA had only ever called `releaseFocus()` directly.
   Movement and commands are now gated separately.
3. **Adjacent rooms' walls stood inside each other.** Walls were placed outside
   their own footprint, so each room's wall protruded into its neighbour. In a
   two-white-room world nobody notices; the moment the demo world put a dark
   green room next to a white one, a green wall appeared floating in the middle
   of the white gallery. Walls now sit inside their own footprint.
4. **The interface hardcoded "Fundación Arenas".** The second world displayed
   the first world's institution and offered its route. Identity and route now
   come from the world record.
5. **Wall anchors sat inside the wall** once walls gained real thickness. The
   Scene Kit now projects them onto the visible surface, so world files stay
   authored against clean room bounds.

## 4. Evidence

`node labs/immersive-worlds/qa/run-qa.mjs` — same runner, extended. New checks:

| Check | Asserts |
|---|---|
| `MEDIA-LOADED` | works load from the institution's own declared files |
| `MEDIA-FALLBACK` | a missing file degrades to a plate and the room survives |
| `WARMUP-COMPILES` | warmup compiles real programs, not an empty scene |
| `DETAIL-NAVIGATION` | next/previous moves between works without leaving detail |
| `DETAIL-ZOOM` | inspection zoom actually moves the camera closer |
| `ESCAPE-IN-FOCUS` | Escape leaves detail while movement input is disabled |
| `DETAIL-RETURN` | after stepping through works, the visitor returns where they stood |
| `SECOND-WORLD` | a different world runs on the same engine and Scene Kit |
| `WORLD-DRIVEN-IDENTITY` | the interface takes its identity from the world |

Captures of every named state, the second world and the authoring surface land in
`labs/immersive-worlds/qa/evidence/`.

Performance figures remain **SwiftShader software rendering** — relative signal
only. The numeric budgets `CONSTITUTION.md` §20 requires still are not set,
because that needs representative hardware.

## 5. Open, and honest

1. **The Gauntlet Loop has still not been run.** No fresh critic, no direct
   comparison against TheVertMenthe or Cartier. The visual step here was made by
   the builder, which the Constitution says is not sufficient. This remains the
   single biggest gap.
2. **No independent Unslop pass.** The reject list was applied while building.
3. **No accessibility audit.** The outline, keyboard paths and reduced-motion
   handling exist and grew in this milestone; an audit does not.
4. **No third-person avatar.** The reference image has one. Constitution treats
   avatars as R&D / SHOULD LATER, so it was deliberately not built. If it is
   wanted for the Museum template, that is a scope decision for Juanma, not one
   an implementation agent should take.
5. **Audio remains synthesised**; no recorded narration.
6. **The dark gallery seen through a doorway** still reads as a near-black
   rectangle from the lit room. Better than IW-1, not yet right.
7. **Barrier lines are derived, not authorable.** An author cannot yet say "no
   rope on this wall" except by moving the work.
8. **`qa/run-qa.mjs` needs `playwright` resolvable** and is still not wired into
   CI, because that would touch shared repository configuration.

### Proposed decisions, not taken unilaterally
